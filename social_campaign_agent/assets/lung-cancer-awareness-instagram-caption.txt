Caption
Lung cancer screening is not for everyone. In the U.S., yearly low-dose CT (LDCT) screening is recommended for adults ages 50-80 with at least a 20 pack-year smoking history who currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years. A pack-year means an average of one pack a day for one year.

Screening can help find lung cancer earlier, but it can also lead to false alarms, unnecessary follow-up procedures, overdiagnosis, and radiation exposure. Talk with a health professional about your personal risk, the benefits and harms, and whether screening is right for you. If you have symptoms, contact a health professional; screening guidance is for people without symptoms.

Sources: CDC, Screening for Lung Cancer (May 12, 2026): https://www.cdc.gov/lung-cancer/screening/index.html ; National Cancer Institute, Lung Cancer Screening PDQ: https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/lung-screening-pdq

General health education, not personal medical advice.
