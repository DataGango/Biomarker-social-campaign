Lung cancer screening is a conversation worth having with a health professional. U.S. guidance recommends yearly low-dose CT screening for adults ages 50-80 who have a 20 pack-year or greater smoking history and currently smoke or quit within the past 15 years. One pack-year equals smoking an average of one pack per day for one year.

Screening may find cancer earlier, but it can also have harms, including false-positive results, follow-up procedures, overdiagnosis, and radiation exposure. Screening is for people without symptoms; if you have symptoms, contact a health professional. This general information is not personal medical advice.
