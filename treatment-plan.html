CARE PLANNING · EDUCATIONAL GUIDE

Understanding a treatment plan.

A treatment plan is specific to the child’s diagnosis and clinical situation. This guide helps families organize questions with the pediatric oncology team; it does not recommend a treatment.

What a care plan can help clarify

Details and timing vary by diagnosis and child.

NCI notes that treatment decisions depend on the type of cancer and other clinical factors. The care team can explain the purpose of each step and how the plan may change as test results and treatment response become available.

01

Confirm the diagnosis

Ask for the exact cancer type and subtype, stage or risk group when applicable, and which test results inform those findings.

02

Understand the goals

Ask what each proposed step is intended to do, how benefit will be assessed, and which uncertainties remain.

03

Review options and tradeoffs

Discuss potential benefits, short- and long-term risks, alternatives, supportive care, and what may happen if plans change.

04

Plan follow-up

Ask who to contact, how urgent symptoms are handled, and what survivorship monitoring or treatment records to keep.

i
Clinical trials are voluntaryNCI says a child is not automatically enrolled because care is provided at a children’s cancer center. Families can discuss a trial’s purpose, possible benefits and risks, and alternatives with the treating team.

Questions to bring to an appointment

Choose what is useful for your conversation.

What is the exact diagnosis and which findings support it?
What is the goal of each part of the proposed plan?
What benefits and risks should we understand?
Which side effects require a call or urgent care?
Are there relevant clinical trials, and what are the alternatives?
Could biomarker or genetic testing change care?
Who coordinates appointments and supportive care?
What should the treatment summary include for future follow-up?
