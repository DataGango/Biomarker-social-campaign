Audience groups to approach (with permission)

Who may be a good fit?

Think in audience segments—not lists of named people. Prioritize people and organizations with a genuine connection to the cause and an easy way to decline.

Community supporters

People who voluntarily follow the campaign organizer, opt into updates, or attend community events.

Approach: share one clear request and an opt-out

Local employers and businesses

Businesses with community-giving or employee-match programs. Ask about eligibility, match caps, deadlines, and approved claims.

Approach: written sponsorship menu and receipt process

Schools, clubs, and civic groups

Groups that choose charitable projects through their own approval process. Protect student privacy and make participation voluntary.

Approach: event proposal with safeguarding details

Foundations and grantmakers

Organizations whose published mission and eligibility match the selected recipient. Confirm deadlines and whether individuals may apply.

Approach: review official guidelines; don't mass-submit

BEFORE LAUNCH

A transparent fundraiser checklist

Verify the beneficiaryConfirm legal name, official donation URL, charitable status, fees, and permitted use of funds.
Set a net goalEstimate platform fees and event costs; distinguish gross donations from funds delivered.
Get story consentUse no patient names, images, diagnosis details, or testimonials without informed permission.
Make claims preciseDon't promise a donation will fund a specific treatment or outcome unless the recipient confirms it.
Plan stewardshipSend receipts through the beneficiary, report totals, and publish a close-out summary.
Keep asks respectfulNo pressure, repeated unwanted requests, scraped leads, or direct-message blasts.
